Official YouTube Bundles in Kenya

Both Safaricom and Airtel have bundles designed for video streaming. However, they are rarely “unlimited” and often come with restrictive Fair Usage Policies (FUP).

  • Safaricom “YouTube” bundle: Sometimes offered as a separate daily or weekly pack (e.g., 1GB for YouTube only). Prices around Ksh 20–50 per day. It only works for YouTube – not for other apps.
  • Airtel “Video” bundles: May include YouTube, Netflix, and other streaming services. Similar pricing, but restricted to video platforms.

These bundles have a major drawback: they do not work if you have Okoa Jahazi debt, and they cannot be used for anything else (browsing, WhatsApp, etc.).

⚠️ Important: Official “YouTube” bundles usually have a hidden data cap. After a few hundred MB, speeds are throttled so much that streaming becomes impossible. Always read the Fair Usage Policy.

How Much Data Does YouTube Really Use?

Data consumption depends on video quality:

  • 144p (lowest): ~50 MB per hour
  • 240p: ~150 MB per hour
  • 360p (good for most phones): ~250 MB per hour
  • 480p: ~400 MB per hour
  • 720p (HD): ~800 MB–1 GB per hour
  • 1080p (Full HD): ~1.5–2 GB per hour

If you stream at 360p for one hour daily, you will need about 250 MB per day, or 7.5 GB per month.

Tips to Save Data While Watching YouTube

  • Lower the resolution – 360p is usually enough for phone screens. You can change it in the video player settings.
  • Download videos over Wi‑Fi – use YouTube’s offline feature (Premium) or download via other tools.
  • Use YouTube Go or YouTube ReVanced – these lite versions consume less data (though official YouTube Go has been discontinued, alternatives exist).
  • Watch during off‑peak hours – networks are less congested, but data consumption remains the same.
  • Limit autoplay – disable autoplay of recommended videos after your chosen video ends.
